Ask your agent for a deck. Present it from a link.

Tell Claude, Cursor or any MCP agent what your talk is about. It builds the slides from 26 ready-made layouts and checks each one in a browser before you see it. You present from a laptop or a phone, and the room joins from their phones to vote, ask questions and react.

Your agent builds it

It fills layouts with your content and never writes HTML. It renders every slide in a browser and fixes overflow and low contrast before you open the deck.

You present from a link

Every deck has a URL. Present from a laptop with your notes, the next slide and a timer, and use your phone as the remote.

The room joins in

Guests scan a QR code, with no app and no account. They answer polls, fill a word cloud, ask and upvote questions, and send reactions.

It looks like your brand

Upload your fonts, logos and colours as a template. Every deck made on it matches, and the editor checks contrast before you publish.

How it works

One sentence in. A finished deck out.

Your agent picks a layout for each point and fills in the text. Then it opens every slide in a browser and measures it, the way your audience will see it. Text that overflows, type that's too small and colours that fail contrast come back as findings, and the agent fixes them before you open the deck.

    Refused before it's saved
    The server checks every slot against its layout. A slide the player can't draw never reaches the deck, and the error names the field to fix.
    
        
    Checked the way a viewer sees it
    check_deck renders each slide and reports overflow, text under 20 px, contrast below WCAG AA, broken images and vague alt text. The deck page shows the same list.
    
        
    A whole deck in one call
    add_slides sends many slides at once, with their notes and poll settings, so a 40-slide deck doesn't take 40 round trips.

    Layouts
    
        
    26 layouts, from a big number to a flow diagram
    
        
    Each slide is a layout with named slots, and your agent fills the slots with plain text. Every layout works in every palette, so a slide always looks finished. Click a slide to see it larger, with the slots the agent filled and its speaker notes. Five more layouts run the live room, and you'll see them further down.

    Templates and palettes
    
        
    Change the palette. Every slide follows.
    
        
    A slide never stores a colour or a font. The palette holds the colours and the template holds the type and shape, so switching either one restyles the whole deck. Every colour pair in a built-in palette passes a contrast check. Pick a palette below to try it.
    
      
    

      
    
        
        
        
        
      
    

      
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    Talk
    talkdefault
    
          
    A story in parts for a room, with one point per slide.
    
          
    1. Title, with the one-line promise
    2. 3–5 parts, each opening with a divider
    3. 2–4 slides per part, one point each
    4. Close on the single ask
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Workshop
    workshopdeep dives on
    
          
    A talk with exercises for the room. Exercises are deep dives you can skip when you run late.
    
          
    1. Title
    2. Per part: a divider, then one practice slide per idea
    3. Exercises as deep dives at the end of their part
    4. Close with the first step for Monday
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Update
    updateno parts
    
          
    A status or metrics report. Numbers first, then what is next.
    
          
    1. Title, with the headline number
    2. The 1–3 numbers that moved, each explained
    3. What is late or blocked, and why
    4. Next steps with owners

      Publish checklist
      
            
        
          
        
        
        
      
        

      
        
        
        Versions that stay put
        Publishing makes a version that never changes. A deck stays on the version it was made with, and its owner is offered the update.
        
        
        Share it like a deck
        Keep a template private, share it by link, or name the people who can use it. A deck always renders, even for viewers the template isn't shared with.
        
        
        Agents can make one too
        create_template, upload_asset and publish_template give an agent the editor's controls and the same checks.

        A presenter view, and your phone as the remote

            A filter for bad words
            A profane word is refused before it's stored. Anything else you don't want on screen, you hide with one key.

            "Scary", "scary " and "SCARY" count as one word, so the cloud shows what the room agrees on.

            Q&A and reactions
            
        
            Questions from the room, and five reactions
            
        
            Guests ask and upvote on a Questions tab, so the question most people want answered rises to the top. Five reactions sit under the slide and float up the big screen. Nothing the room writes reaches the big screen until you show it. Upvote or ask on the phone, tap a reaction on its Slide tab, and press Show on screen in the panel.

                You decide what goes on screen
                Show on screen puts one question over the slide, and a Q&A slide lists the top five. Hide a question, or have new ones wait for your approval.
                
        
                Anonymous unless they say so
                Questions have no name by default. A guest can sign a single question, and you can turn names off.
                
        
                Answer the rest later
                After the talk, get_questions gives your agent every question with its votes, so it can draft answers to the ones you didn't get to.

                Self-hosted and MIT licensed
                
          
                Run it yourself. Your decks stay on your server.
                
          
                SlideWorks is one container with a SQLite database inside. Put it behind your TLS proxy, sign in with Google, and point any MCP client at it. Your decks, polls and questions never leave your server.
