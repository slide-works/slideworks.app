The problem

You get about seven minutes before the room drifts

It's an old rule of presenting. Around seven minutes in, people reach for their phones, looking out a window, thinking about their cats, and from then on you're talking to the tops of heads. Most presenters fight back with things like "hands up if you've…", and the same few hands go up while everyone else keeps scrolling.

"Hands up" hears from a few

The confident front row answers and the rest stay quiet. A poll on every phone hears from the whole room, and nobody sees who picked what.

Questions wait until the end

By then most are forgotten or feel too small to ask. A Questions tab takes them at any point, and you can answer one on the slide it's about or save them all for the end.

You can't see who's lost

Nods look the same from the front. A word cloud or a quick poll shows you what the room thinks while there's still time to change course.

The phone they reach for at minute seven becomes the way they take part.

Your phone runs the talk

No clicker to carry, and no podium to stand behind looking down at a laptop. Your phone moves the slides and keeps your notes in your hand, so you can walk the room.

The room joins in ten seconds

Guests point their camera at a QR code. There's no app to install, no account and no name to give.

Everyone gets a say

Polls, word clouds, open questions, upvoted Q&A and reactions, live on the big screen while you talk.

Your agent builds the deck

Give it your notes and numbers. It builds the slides with the polls in place and checks every one before you open it.

Presenting

Run the talk from your phone

Speaker notes on a laptop keep you at the podium, looking down at a screen, and a clicker only moves the slides. Scan a QR code and your phone does both. It moves the slides and shows your notes and the next slide, so you can walk the room and still know what comes next. It runs the polls and the question list too. On the laptop, the presenter window gives you the same in a dark view, with a timer that turns amber with five minutes left. Click the window and press the arrow keys or B, or tap Next on the phone.

Live polls

Ask the room, and watch the bars move

"Hands up" only hears from people happy to be seen. A poll hears from everyone, privately. It's a slide your agent writes like any other, and when you reach it, phones show the question, and the bars on the big screen grow as answers arrive. Tap an option on the phone, then close the poll, show the answer or start a new round.

    One answer or several

    Up to 8 options, pick one or pick a few. Mark a correct answer and reveal it after you close the poll. Every round is kept.

    Honest answers

    Guests have no name and no account, and nobody can see who picked what. Show results live, once a guest has answered, or after the poll closes.

    Your agent reads the results

    After the talk, get_poll_results gives your agent each round's counts and response rate, ready for the follow-up email.

    Word clouds and open text

    Let the room answer in its own words

    Multiple choice only gives back the answers you thought of. Ask an open question and each guest sends up to three words. The words people repeat grow on the big screen as they arrive. For longer replies, an open-text slide shows each answer as a card on a wall. Type a word on the phone and send it to add yours.

          In the moment or at the end

          Show puts one question over any slide, from your phone or the presenter window, and a Q&A slide lists the top five. Nothing reaches the big screen until you show it.

          Anonymous unless they say so

          Questions have no name by default. A guest can sign a single question, and you can turn names off.

          After the talk, get_questions gives your agent every question with its votes, so it can draft answers to the ones you didn't get to.

          Every language in the room

          Your slides and your words, in each guest's own language

          You give the talk in one language, but the room often speaks several. People following in their second language take in less, and they rarely tell you. You choose the deck's languages from 16, including Thai, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Malay, Indonesian, Mandarin and Cantonese. Each guest picks theirs on their own phone.

          3In their ears

          They listen in their own language

          A guest taps Listen in the subtitle strip, puts on headphones, and hears each translated line read by their phone's own voice. Turn the phone sideways and the slide fills the screen with the lines over it, like a film. Press Hear it to hear a line in your browser.

          • Always close to live. If listening falls more than three lines behind, it jumps to the newest and says how many it skipped.
          • Keeps going with the screen off in the SlideWorks app, with Pause and Jump to live on the lock screen.
          • In the app, it pauses when the headphones come out, so a phone never starts talking in the middle of the room.
          • Choose the voice and speed. Traditional Chinese readers choose Cantonese or Mandarin.

          Translated on your server

          Open-weight translation models run on your own server's CPU, with no cloud service. An admin page picks the model for each pair of languages and lets you try them side by side.

          Speech stays on the speaker's phone

          The speech model runs on the phone. No audio is uploaded or stored, and the transcript is kept only if you turn that on.

          Your agent translates the slides

          translate_slides writes a language and checks that every line still fits, in that language's font. check_deck then renders each language and reports what doesn't fit.

          Your agent

          Spend your time on what to say, not on moving boxes

          You bring the outline, the numbers and the point you want the room to leave with. Your agent picks a layout for each point, adds the polls and word clouds you asked for, and checks every slide in a browser before you open the deck. After the talk, it reads back the votes and questions for your follow-up.

            Refused before it's saved
            The server checks every slot against its layout. A slide the player can't draw never reaches the deck, and the error names the field to fix.
            
        
            Checked the way a viewer sees it
            check_deck renders each slide and reports overflow, text under 20 px, contrast below WCAG AA, broken images and vague alt text. The deck page shows the same list.
            
        
            A whole deck in one call
            add_slides sends many slides at once, with their notes and poll settings, so a 40-slide deck doesn't take 40 round trips.

            Talk
            talkdefault
            
          
            A story in parts for a room, with one point per slide.
            
          
            1. Title, with the one-line promise
            2. 3–5 parts, each opening with a divider
            3. 2–4 slides per part, one point each
            4. Close on the single ask
            
        
            
        
            
          
            Workshop
            workshopdeep dives on
            
          
            A talk with exercises for the room. Exercises are deep dives you can skip when you run late.
            
          
            1. Title
            2. Per part: a divider, then one practice slide per idea
            3. Exercises as deep dives at the end of their part
            4. Close with the first step for Monday
            
        
            
        
            
          
            Update
            updateno parts
            
          
            A status or metrics report. Numbers first, then what is next.
            
          
            1. Title, with the headline number
            2. The 1–3 numbers that moved, each explained
            3. What is late or blocked, and why
            4. Next steps with owners
