Agents build the deck

An MCP server gives Claude Code, Cursor and other agents tools to create a deck, add slides from typed building blocks, and fix what the server rejects.

Present from a URL

Every deck has a link. Open it in a browser and present on a laptop or a phone, with speaker notes and a part tracker that shows where you are in the talk.

Bring the audience in

Coming later: people join with a QR code and answer live polls, word clouds and Q&A from their phones while you drive the deck from yours.

How it works

From a brief to a deck, one tool call at a time

The agent writes no HTML. It reads the layout catalogue, then sends each slide as a layout and its slots. The server checks every slot against the layout's schema and rejects a slide it can't render, naming the field that broke, so the agent can fix it and try again.

  1. 1
    Connect your agentPoint it at /mcp. It registers itself, and you approve it in the browser.
  2. 2
    Give it a brief"A 20-minute offsite talk on shipping smaller." It picks a template and palette and builds the slides.
  3. 3
    Open the linkReview it on the deck page, tweak the notes, share it, and present.

    Building blocks
    
        
    17 blocks. Zero HTML.
    
        
    A slide is a layout plus typed slots. Here is every block in one 16-slide deck: one sentence per headline, light content slides with cards, dark slides for the title, dividers and close, and a part tracker across the top. Under each slide are the slots an agent fills.
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Templates and palettes
    
        
    A template and a palette. That's the whole look.
    
        
    The template holds the structure, the type and the shape. The palette holds every colour. A slide never stores a colour or a font, so the same deck looks right in any palette. Every built-in palette passes a contrast check, and the build fails if a pair drops below its bar.
    
      
    

      
    
        
        
        
        
      
    

      
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    Talk
    talkdefault
    
          
    A talk to a room: a story in parts, with one point per slide.
    
          
    1. Title, with the one-line promise
    2. 3–5 parts, each opening with a divider
    3. 2–4 slides per part, one point each
    4. Close on the single ask
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Workshop
    workshopdeep dives on
    
          
    A talk plus exercises the room does. Exercises are deep dives you can skip when you run late.
    
          
    1. Title
    2. Per part: a divider, then one practice slide per idea
    3. Exercises as deep dives at the end of their part
    4. Close with the first step for Monday
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Update
    updateno parts
    
          
    A status or metrics report. Numbers first, then what is next.
    
          
    1. Title, with the headline number
    2. The 1–3 numbers that moved, each explained
    3. What is late or blocked, and why
    4. Next steps with owners
    
        
    
      
    
      
    Later: all 348 Sanzo Wada colour combinations as palettes, and your own templates, uploaded or imported from an HTML deck.
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    
        
    The app
    
        
    Every deck in one ledger, and who changed it
    
        
    A table, not a wall of thumbnails, so forty decks stay easy to scan. The Last change column says whether an agent or a person made it, and the deck page's activity log shows who changed what, when, and through which tool.

    Private until you share
    Keep a deck private, share it with anyone who has the link, or name the people who can view it. Agents get the same control through set_sharing.
    
        
    An activity log on every deck
    Every change is logged with who made it and through which tool, so you can see what your agent did while you were away.
    
        
    Checked before it's saved
    Every slot is validated against its layout's schema. A slide the player can't render never reaches the deck.
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Designed · next up
    
        
    A presenter view, and your phone as the remote
    
        
    The presenter window is dark, so it doesn't light up your face from the lectern. The current slide is largest, the next one waits beside your notes, and the timer turns amber with five minutes left. Scan a QR code and your phone drives the same talk. Try it: click into the window and use ← → and B, or tap Next on the phone.

    Roadmap
    
        
    Built in the open, one slice at a time
    
        
    Every Feature starts as reviewed mockups with its design questions answered, then ships in small pieces. The mockups on this page come straight from those designs.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Built
    
          
    SW-1Foundation
    Deck library, deck page, sharing, the player, the 17 blocks and the MCP server.
    
          
    SW-2Templates and palettes
    Talk, workshop and update templates. Cobalt, Ember, Plum and Midnight. Share cards.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Designed
    
          
    SW-4Presenter view and phone remote
    Current and next slide, notes, timer, blackout, skipping deep dives, and your phone as the remote.
    
          
    SW-5Audience join
    A QR code and a six-digit code. Guests follow the slides with no app and no account.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Planned
    
          
    SW-6+Live audience activities
    Polls, word clouds and Q&A, answered from phones in the room.
    
          
    SW-15Sanzo Wada palettes
    All 348 classic colour combinations, mapped to roles with contrast fixes.
    
          
    SW-50Your own templates
    Upload a template, or import one from an existing HTML deck.
    
        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Self-hosted and MIT licensed
    
          
    Run it yourself. Your decks stay yours.
    
          
    One container: ASP.NET Core and a React player, with SQLite for storage. Put it behind your TLS proxy, sign in with Google, and connect any MCP client to it.
